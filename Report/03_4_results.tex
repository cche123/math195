\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsubsection{Prediction of valence and arousal}

Table~\ref{tab:performance} reports pooled out-of-fold results for the 3,542-recording follow-up. The broader sample supports prediction of both valence and arousal.

The seven-feature ridge model obtains valence $R^2=0.090$ and arousal $R^2=0.304$. Expanding the representation increases these values to $0.213$ and $0.415$. Because these models use the same recordings and outer groups, this comparison provides evidence that the added measurements improve prediction within the evaluation.

The random forest gives the strongest performance among the evaluated configurations. Its valence RMSE is approximately 16\% below the training-mean baseline, while its arousal RMSE is approximately 29\% below the baseline. Inner validation selected the forest in every primary outer fold for both responses.

The small LSTM produces positive held-out $R^2$ values but does not improve on the expanded static models. This finding concerns the evaluated architecture, training settings, and four-feature sequence representation. Further comparisons would be needed to assess other sequence models or richer window-level inputs.

The earlier pilot's weak valence result therefore does not establish that valence is unpredictable from audio. The broader sample and expanded representation support held-out prediction. However, the pilot-to-follow-up difference cannot be attributed entirely to the feature expansion, since sample size and composition also changed.

\subsubsection{Contribution of acoustic feature groups}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports changes in expanded-ridge RMSE after removing each feature family and repeating tuning and evaluation.

Removing the added timbre measurements increases valence RMSE by approximately $0.0062$. Removing pitch-class and triad measurements increases it by approximately $0.0028$. These groups therefore provide information beyond the remaining measurements in this model.

Small changes after removing an original feature group do not establish that the corresponding musical property is unimportant. The expanded representation contains correlated measurements that may preserve similar information. The groups also differ in size, so their removal effects should be interpreted within the selected representation.

The descriptive major-minus-minor triad contrast has a positive valence coefficient in all five primary ridge folds. This provides a specific pitch-related quantity to investigate in the case studies. Its interpretation remains limited by the feature definition: it describes a template match rather than a confirmed key or chord progression.

\subsubsection{Sensitivity to group assignment}

Expanded ridge was evaluated using three group-split seeds. Valence $R^2$ ranged from approximately $0.207$ to $0.213$, and arousal $R^2$ ranged from approximately $0.412$ to $0.415$. Its RMSE remained below the corresponding training-mean baseline in each evaluation.

\subsubsection{Interpretation and limitations}

The results support a concrete answer within the evaluated MERGE sample: acoustic measurements contain predictive information about both positivity and activation, and the expanded timbre and pitch-class representation improves valence prediction over the original seven features.

The stronger arousal scores suggest that the evaluated representations capture activation more completely than positivity. The scores describe prediction of supplied numerical labels; they are not percentages of listeners whose emotions were identified correctly.

Several limitations remain. The follow-up was designed after examining the pilot and overlaps that sample. No untouched external dataset has been evaluated, so these are exploratory cross-validation findings. Artist-credit grouping also does not resolve every alias or collaboration.

The supplied labels originate from MERGE's annotation process rather than new ratings collected for this project. In addition, feature contributions and removal comparisons describe associations within fitted models. Those associations may reflect genre, production, instrumentation, or other correlated information.
