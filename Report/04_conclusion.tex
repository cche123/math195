\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The cross-genre analysis finds predictive information for both valence and arousal in measurable acoustic features. Expanded ridge regression obtains held-out $R^2=0.213$ for valence and $R^2=0.415$ for arousal, with similar results under three artist-group assignments. The random forest improves prediction further, reaching $R^2=0.293$ and $R^2=0.494$ in the primary evaluation.

The expanded representation provides a more useful account of emotional positivity than the original seven features on the same broader sample. Removing added timbre or pitch-class measurements increases valence prediction error. A descriptive major-versus-minor triad feature also retains a positive coefficient across the primary ridge folds, giving us a specific tonal measurement to examine alongside timbre, amplitude, and rhythm.

These findings establish a stronger basis for the case studies than the original pilot alone. We can compare predicted positivity and activation for ``Apocalypse'' by Cigarettes After Sex and ``Happy'' by Pharrell Williams, trace the ridge predictions to measured features, and examine how those contributions differ across musical sections.

The interpretation remains limited to the supplied labels and evaluated sample. The models leave substantial variation unexplained, and the present results do not identify universal causes of musical emotion. The two-song analysis will therefore examine both agreement and disagreement with listening observations.

The remaining work is to complete the data-collection account, review the eligibility decisions with the final project dataset, and apply the fitted models to the selected recordings. The completed analysis nevertheless provides a concrete preliminary answer: timbre and pitch-related information improve prediction of emotional positivity, while activation is predicted more strongly by the evaluated acoustic representations.
